**Gas City versus SASE: where Gas City is ahead, and what SASE should borrow**

Independent research by **cdx**, 2026-10-17, for the `research.1x` swarm.

**Main finding.** Gas City's strongest advantage is that it treats a multi-agent job as a continuously operated work graph. Its controller can advance the graph, dispatch ready work, evaluate checks, create bounded retry attempts, and reconcile workers independently of the session that requested the job. SASE already has many of the ingredients: durable beads, dependency scheduling, parallel workflows, supervision, gates, continuations, and host-owned finalizers. Its opportunity is to make those ingredients behave as one recoverable execution system.

This is a comparison of implemented mechanisms and product design, **not a measured claim that Gas City produces better code, costs less, or fails less often**. I would borrow several mechanisms, but would not replace SASE with Gas City on this evidence.

The highest-value changes are recoverable workflow execution, explicit task-failure policy, and explanations of why work is waiting. Pools, portable orchestration bundles, and broader APIs become more useful after those foundations are dependable.

**Comparison at a glance.** An advantage below applies to the stated use, rather than every dimension of either product.

| Dimension | Gas City's advantage | SASE already provides | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Durable workflow operation | Controller-owned control nodes and recorded attempts | Parallel workflows, dependency waves, durable handoffs, epic resume | Strongest gap: general crash recovery and uniform execution policy |
| Task and worker recovery | Work checks/retries plus configured reconciliation | Provider retries, update-skew recovery, service supervision, manual retry | Gas City is broader; superior uptime is unproven |
| Backlog-driven staffing | Work routing separate from session identity; declarative pools | Weighted admission, waits, queued launches, scheduler proposals | Valuable for ongoing queues; less important for finite swarms |
| Operational explanation | Reconciler decision traces and a durable event feed | TUI, run artifacts, telemetry, gateway SSE | Advantage in explaining decisions and supporting external consumers |
| Reusable orchestration | Packs, namespaced imports, pins, deployment separation | Plugins, configurable macros/jobs/finalizers, overrides | Packaging advantage, not an absence of extensibility |
| Agent communication | Addressed mail, threads, read/archive state | Artifacts, variables, bead notes, continuations | Useful for cooperating sessions, inappropriate for independent swarms |
| Hosting and clients | Runtime interface, Kubernetes/exec backends, browser dashboard | Headless adapters, remote dispatch, Rust gateway, mobile/TUI | Broader surface; conditional value for SASE |
| Typed inputs and human decisions | Some formula declarations remain inert | Typed workflow inputs/outputs and durable command-backed gates | SASE has strengths to preserve |

**The durable work graph is the clearest advantage.**

Gas City's v2 formula compiler produces work nodes and controller-owned control nodes. The dispatcher implements checks, retries, fan-out, drain, scope handling, and finalization. For example, `processAttemptControl` reads the latest attempt, classifies its result, records the attempt log, then passes, fails, exhausts the budget, or creates another attempt. The running controller has real dispatch wiring. This is more than a design proposal. See the [formula specification](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/docs/reference/specs/formula-spec-v2.md), [attempt controller](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/internal/dispatch/control.go), and [dispatch runtime](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/cmd/gc/dispatch_runtime.go).

Consider a job with implementation, three independent reviews, synthesis, and a check-and-revise loop. Gas City explicitly represents unfinished work and the attempts already spent. A worker session executes part of that job; it need not be the only place where the next action is known.

SASE can already express much of this. Its [workflow specification](https://github.com/sase-org/sase/blob/5ea5fa5452c863ce2bd01b034fcc87b44ae83095/docs/reference/workflows.md) includes agent/script steps, parallel execution, typed outputs, loops, joins, cleanup, and human approval. Its [bead-work machinery](https://github.com/sase-org/sase/blob/5ea5fa5452c863ce2bd01b034fcc87b44ae83095/docs/reference/work-units/bead-work.md) constructs dependency waves, checkpoints epic graphs, skips completed/delegated phases on retry, and schedules a land agent. Calling SASE merely a prompt-expansion tool would be false.

The narrower gap is general recovery after an **unexpected interruption**. `WorkflowExecutor` initializes a fresh `WorkflowState` and iterates from the first step. It can skip steps when outputs are supplied as arguments, but that is not a general controller adopting a crashed workflow from persisted execution history. Intentional HITL and monitor handoffs have dedicated continuation paths and must not be confused with this gap. The bead-work documentation explicitly tells the operator to retry or close a phase that crashes before closure. See the [executor](https://github.com/sase-org/sase/blob/5ea5fa5452c863ce2bd01b034fcc87b44ae83095/src/sase/macro/workflow_executor.py) and [HITL continuation behavior](https://github.com/sase-org/sase/blob/5ea5fa5452c863ce2bd01b034fcc87b44ae83095/docs/reference/workflows.md#human-in-the-loop).

**Inference:** SASE's execution capabilities are spread across several local mechanisms; Gas City makes durable graph advancement a central responsibility. The useful improvement is to unify recovery semantics, not add a second workflow language or replace YAML with TOML.

**Task-level retry and worker reconciliation are broader in Gas City.**

Three failures need different treatment: a provider request fails, a worker disappears or stalls, or a worker completes an attempt that fails an acceptance check. Gas City's formula `check` and `retry` controls put attempt limits and terminal dispositions into the work graph. The specification distinguishes a failed check from some infrastructure failures that prevent a check from running. Worker reconciliation separately addresses desired sessions, runtime observations, configuration drift, restart limits, and idle handling. See [check/retry semantics](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/docs/reference/specs/formula-spec-v2.md#3-runtime), [control implementation](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/internal/dispatch/control.go), and [session reconciliation](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/cmd/gc/session_reconciler.go).

SASE already retries provider/transport failures, supports fallback models, supervises service processes, and has carefully bounded recovery for live-update skew. Its auto-restart documentation explicitly excludes general tool/test failures, provider errors, OOM/timeouts, and ordinary post-provider failures from that particular service. This is a prudent boundary, not evidence that SASE has no retries. I did not find a similarly general work-level policy joining acceptance checks, failed-attempt evidence, retry budgets, and downstream disposition. See [provider retries](https://github.com/sase-org/sase/blob/5ea5fa5452c863ce2bd01b034fcc87b44ae83095/docs/reference/providers/retry-and-fallback.md) and [auto-restart boundaries](https://github.com/sase-org/sase/blob/5ea5fa5452c863ce2bd01b034fcc87b44ae83095/docs/reference/agent-auto-restart.md).

For SASE, distinguish a failed acceptance check, failure to obtain a usable runtime, intentional cancellation, and an uncertain external outcome. They should not consume the same budget or trigger the same action. Silence alone is weak evidence: a compiler, a rate-limit wait, and a human gate can all be quiet without being broken.

Gas City's broader policy does **not** prove superior uptime. Its [1.6 reliability issue](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/issues/7093) still names autonomous convergence and replacing the legacy reconciler as goals. At the inspected revision, `legacy` remains the default. The new reconciler performs real effects when admitted, but its latch rejects deferred features including `scale_check` and some dependency/timeout features. See [reconciler documentation](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/engdocs/architecture/reconciler-v2.md) and [mode selection](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/cmd/gc/reconcile_mode.go). **Formula v2 and session reconciler v2 are different switches**; availability of the former does not imply the latter is production-default.

**Pools better express continuous, backlog-driven work.**

Gas City separates routing to an agent template from assigning a concrete session. Pool policy includes minimum/maximum active sessions and demand queries. The controller accounts for sessions already owning actionable work and separately incorporates new demand. Current fields include `min_active_sessions`, `max_active_sessions`, and `scale_check`; older design examples using `[agent.pool]` should not be copied as current syntax. See [pool evaluation](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/cmd/gc/pool.go), [desired-state calculation](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/cmd/gc/pool_desired_state.go), and [current pack examples](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/docs/guides/shareable-packs.md).

This directly expresses “keep at most two reviewers working whenever approved reviews are ready,” without requiring an operator to manage each launch. It also gives the staffing policy a direct relationship to the queue.

SASE's `%queue`, priorities, weights, waits, provider usage policies, and scheduler launch proposals already address capacity and automation. A custom scheduler job could implement a dispatcher today. Gas City's advantage is a standard, inspectable work-to-executor contract with ownership and recovery, rather than raw concurrency. See [SASE launch directives](https://github.com/sase-org/sase/blob/5ea5fa5452c863ce2bd01b034fcc87b44ae83095/docs/reference/macros/directive-launch.md), [scheduler jobs](https://github.com/sase-org/sase/blob/5ea5fa5452c863ce2bd01b034fcc87b44ae83095/docs/reference/automation/scheduler-jobs.md), and [admission predicates](https://github.com/sase-org/sase/blob/5ea5fa5452c863ce2bd01b034fcc87b44ae83095/src/sase/core/runner_slots/_admission_predicates.py).

For a finite research swarm, the incremental value is modest. For a steady stream of reviews, CI repairs, or approved maintenance tasks, it is larger. SASE can retain single-turn workers: a pool can be a bounded supply of disposable turns, not indefinitely running chats.

**Gas City makes control-plane decisions easier to investigate.**

`gc trace` addresses why a session started, did not start, drained, or restarted. It exposes decision reasons, operations, effective configuration/provenance, and cycle identifiers. Even “no demand” can be recorded as a positive decision when detailed tracing is enabled. That distinguishes correct inactivity from a broken reconciler. See [reconciler debugging](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/engdocs/contributors/reconciler-debugging.md).

Gas City's event interface combines JSONL, HTTP reads, SSE, cursors, filtering, and typed payloads. Its API covers work, sessions, formulas, orders, mail, configuration, and packs. Named tests enforce OpenAPI and payload consistency. The built-in browser dashboard consumes the same API. See [events](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/docs/reference/events.md), [API](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/docs/reference/api.md), [API invariants](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/internal/api/AGENTS.md), and [dashboard](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/docs/getting-started/dashboard.md).

SASE has substantial observability: its TUI, artifacts, launch timing, ToolRuns, telemetry, and mobile/fleet gateway. It has SSE with reconnect IDs, too. The documented mobile buffer is in memory; clients resynchronize after restart or overflow. Core routes expose agent control, bead reads, notifications, gates, and fleet operations. The fair advantage is **broader orchestration coverage and dedicated causal diagnostics**, not the mere existence of an API. See [SASE gateway contract](https://github.com/sase-org/sase/blob/5ea5fa5452c863ce2bd01b034fcc87b44ae83095/docs/contribute/mobile-gateway-api.md) and [core routes](https://github.com/sase-org/sase-core/blob/b0cd62b931d9d637a3fa9ede1e7d5b2cf9cf02eb/crates/sase_gateway/src/routes/router.rs).

For Bryan, a trustworthy “why is this blocked?” view is likely more useful than a second dashboard. It should name the dependency, gate, quota, hold, lease, failed predecessor, or uncertain observation, with the last decision time and next event that could change it. The same backend projection should serve CLI, TUI, and external clients.

**Reusable orchestration has a clearer packaging boundary.**

A Gas City pack combines agents, prompts, formulas, orders, commands, doctor checks, and supporting files. Named imports provide namespaces; sources can be pinned and lockfile-resolved. `pack.toml` defines reusable behavior, `city.toml` specifies deployment choices, and machine-local bindings are separate. These are useful boundaries for sharing a complete review or maintenance process. See [pack concepts](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/docs/guides/understanding-packs.md), [authoring](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/docs/guides/shareable-packs.md), and [import tests](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/internal/config/import_test.go).

SASE plugins already distribute macros/configuration and extend providers, workspaces, VCS, artifacts, commands, and finalizers. Project/home macros override bundled behavior. There is no basis for describing SASE as unconfigurable. See [plugin API](https://github.com/sase-org/sase/blob/5ea5fa5452c863ce2bd01b034fcc87b44ae83095/docs/contribute/plugin-api.md) and [macro discovery](https://github.com/sase-org/sase/blob/5ea5fa5452c863ce2bd01b034fcc87b44ae83095/docs/reference/macros/discovery.md).

Gas City's advantage is the cohesive authoring story for a whole orchestration and its separation from deployment. SASE should demonstrate this through its existing plugin system: install a pinned workflow, choose local providers/capacity, inspect resolved configuration, and run without copying templates. A competing pack subsystem or new registry would add maintenance before proving the need.

Keep role-specific behavior in configuration and prompts, while retaining deterministic safety policy in the host. SASE's Rust-core rule already provides a shared-domain boundary. Gas City's prohibition on hardcoded roles is useful discipline; it is not a reason to move concurrency invariants, completion checks, or approvals into model prompts.

**Durable addressed mail is a distinct capability.**

Gas City's mail contract includes sender/recipient identity, unread/read state, threads, replies, archive operations, and stable session-ID metadata for routing across display-name changes. Providers share conformance tests. This supports clarification requests and information left for later sessions. See [mail contract](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/internal/mail/mail.go) and [conformance tests](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/internal/mail/mailtest/conformance.go).

SASE passes information through artifacts, variables, bead notes, dependencies, forks, and continuations. Those cover planned pipelines. A mailbox would help when the recipient and delivery time are not known at workflow-authoring time; it would not justify universal agent chatter or a persistent coordinator model.

Delivery at the recipient's next authorized turn fits SASE. Messages should be bounded, reference large artifacts, and record read/expiry state. They must not silently start model turns or violate independent-swarm isolation. Gas City's optional send deduplication is itself documented as best-effort and non-atomic; a mailbox is not an exactly-once delivery guarantee.

**Runtime and browser surfaces are broader, but less urgent to borrow.**

Gas City separates the coding harness from its hosting runtime. The tree contains tmux, subprocess, exec, ACP, and Kubernetes machinery. Its exec-provider contract supports an out-of-process implementation, giving hosting changes a boundary separate from coding-model adapters. See [runtime interface](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/internal/runtime/runtime.go), [exec protocol](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/docs/reference/exec-session-provider.md), and [Kubernetes provider](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/internal/runtime/k8s/provider.go).

SASE already runs headless providers and dispatches to enrolled remote machines with operation receipts and remote controls. Remote execution is not missing. The possible improvement is a uniform contract for execution identity, capabilities, workspaces, operation outcomes, and observation uncertainty across backends. See [remote dispatch](https://github.com/sase-org/sase/blob/5ea5fa5452c863ce2bd01b034fcc87b44ae83095/docs/reference/integrations/remote-dispatch.md).

Do not prioritize Kubernetes for its own sake. Gas City's [100-pod example](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/examples/hyperscale/city.toml) explicitly describes workers that claim a bead, mark it done, and exit. It demonstrates scaling machinery, not 100 productive coding agents. The dashboard is documented for local single-operator use. Neither it nor imported packs establishes an enterprise security boundary: the [trust model](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/docs/reference/trust-boundaries.md) treats packs and configuration as trusted executable code.

**Its crash-injection testing is especially worth borrowing.**

The dispatcher's [crash-injection harness](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/internal/dispatch/crash_injection_test.go) records an uninterrupted run, then injects failure at each store-write position, lets subsequent cycles converge, and compares the final ledger with the uninterrupted result. Another cycle must make no further changes. Fixtures cover retry, fan-out, drain, and workflow finalization.

This is stronger evidence for a recovery contract than a happy-path demonstration. Its limits matter: it does not prove arbitrary external effects are idempotent, all storage backends behave identically, or model answers are correct. I inspected rather than executed it.

SASE already has recovery, continuation, finalizer, and contract tests. I am not claiming fault-injection testing is absent everywhere. Apply this systematic method to the cross-boundary operations being extended: graph creation, claim, launch, gate/monitor handoff, result adoption, and finalization. Make it an acceptance criterion for recovery work, not a separate testing rewrite.

**Claims I would not carry forward.**

- **Gas City's formula language is uniformly stronger.** Its [specification lists accepted-but-inert features](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/blob/45c804d59ecc8844eacdeb7b1a3ff263dda7e689/docs/reference/specs/formula-spec-v2.md#4-accepted-but-inert): `until` re-execution, bundled watchers for named gate types, the distinction between `waits_for` modes, and variable `type` enforcement. A gate bead can block a dependency; the missing part is automated interpretation. SASE's typed inputs/outputs and durable human decisions are strengths.
- **Dolt should replace SASE's storage.** Gas City's [performance priority](https://github.com/gastownhall/gascity/issues/7094) moves infrastructure bookkeeping toward SQLite. Separate high-churn runtime state from durable work history and measure hot paths. SASE already uses different stores for different domains.
- **Worktrees would solve SASE workspace overhead.** SASE reuses workspaces and shares Git objects through alternates. Require startup/disk measurements, rather than inferring overhead from the word clone. See [SASE workspaces](https://github.com/sase-org/sase/blob/5ea5fa5452c863ce2bd01b034fcc87b44ae83095/docs/reference/agents/workspaces.md).
- **Adopt Gas Town's role hierarchy.** Gas City's advance is configurable role behavior. There is no reason to import Mayors, Polecats, or LLM patrols for mechanical supervision.
- **Persistence implies correctness or exactly-once effects.** Neither follows. Acceptance checks, uncertain-outcome handling, and ownership remain necessary.
- **Gas City proves a better merge queue.** This investigation did not establish that. The prior Gas Town synthesis rejected an advertised batch/bisect claim as unwired. Evaluate SASE landing discipline separately rather than inheriting an unverified advantage.

**Validation before expansion.** Use a small workload on the same providers and task corpus: independent tasks, a dependency chain across two repositories, a failed acceptance check, a human gate, and conflicting integration. Inject interruption after claim but before launch acknowledgement, during a step, after result persistence, and during finalization. Include a legitimate rate-limit wait and intentional cancellation.

Measure operator interventions per completed deliverable, ready-to-start latency, retry cost, duplicate launches, lost work, finished-to-integrated latency, and the fraction of blocked states with a specific explanation. The target is fewer interventions at unchanged or better correctness. Agent count and PR volume alone are insufficient. Start with SASE's baseline; run a live Gas City comparison if adopting it remains a real decision.

**Ranked improvements to consider.** This order weighs operator value, comparative evidence, implementation cost, and dependencies. Shared domain behavior belongs in `sase-core`; Python and TUI code should consume it. Preserve single-turn agents, host-owned completion, durable gates, and human settlement of goals.

1. **Add recoverable execution to existing workflows and epics.** Highest value; larger change. Record stable step/attempt identity, workflow/configuration version, inputs, outputs, and adoption state. Reuse continuation/finalizer records. Require rerun policy for side-effecting steps; inspect uncertain publish outcomes rather than blindly repeating them. Start with a small resumable subset and crash tests at each durable boundary. **Success:** tested executor interruptions neither lose completed work nor repeat known completed external actions.

2. **Introduce bounded work-level failure policy.** High value; medium-to-large change, building on the first item. Distinguish runtime unavailability, worker loss, acceptance failure, cancellation, and uncertain effects. Preserve failed-attempt evidence and apply attempt/time/cost bounds where observable, backoff, and escalation. Gather progress signals before automatically restarting quiet workers. Reuse provider retries and storm protection. **Success:** a failed phase recovers within policy or reaches an explained terminal state without silently stranding unrelated work.

3. **Expose a causal “why waiting?” view before another dashboard.** High practical value; medium change, possible to start now. Join dependency, capacity, quota, hold, gate, ownership, recovery, and observation state into one response with reasons, evidence time, and the next expected transition. Share it across CLI/TUI and retain bounded history. Keep unknown observations distinct from absent workers. **Success:** an operator identifies a stalled deliverable's cause without opening several unrelated logs.

4. **Standardize backlog-driven dispatch with explicit authorization and budgets.** High value for recurring queues; medium change after ownership/recovery are sound. Start with one approved queue and map ready work to role templates. Apply project/provider limits, stable claims, cancellation rules, and bounded launch policy using single-turn workers and current admission controls. Do not automatically execute every newly filed bead. **Success:** approved work starts when capacity appears and dispatcher restarts do not double-launch it.

5. **Package one complete orchestration through SASE's existing plugins.** Medium value; smaller-to-medium change. Ship a review-and-revise workflow with prompts, checks, scheduler policy, compatibility requirements, and deployment examples. Expose resolved configuration and pin dependencies where needed. Separate reusable behavior from machine paths and capacity preferences. Add finer overrides only when the example demonstrates the need. **Success:** two projects run the same workflow without copied templates or core changes.

6. **Add a small opt-in session inbox delivered at turn boundaries.** Medium value for cooperating teams; medium change. Start with direct messages, stable recipient identity, artifact references, read/expiry semantics, and delivery records. Defer broadcasts and automatic wakeups. Enforce independent-swarm isolation. **Success:** the recipient's next authorized turn receives its pending clarification once in context, with inspectable delivery evidence.

7. **Broaden the existing gateway around durable work and runs.** Conditional value; medium-to-large change. Extend the Rust gateway with run state, blocked reasons, attempts, dependencies, and CLI-equivalent mutations backed by generated contracts. Add durable event consumption for a demonstrated client with a clear resync contract. A richer browser view should consume those APIs. **Success:** an external client can follow and operate a workflow without scraping the TUI or reading private files.

8. **Add a hosting-runtime contract when a second need is concrete.** Lower immediate priority; potentially large change. Preserve provider adapters while normalizing execution identity, capabilities, workspace transport, observation failures, and operation receipts. Pilot container/exec hosting if isolation or capacity is a measured bottleneck; defer a Kubernetes platform and broad runtime matrix. **Success:** the same single-turn jobs and recovery tests work through two backends without duplicated domain logic.
